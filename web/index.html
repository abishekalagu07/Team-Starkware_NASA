    How to read radar

    NISAR sends its own microwave pulses (L-band 24 cm, S-band 9 cm) and records the echo, so it sees through cloud and smoke, by day and night, every 12 days.

    Dark
    Smooth surfaces mirror the pulse away: calm water, tarmac, flooded paddies.
    Green (HV)
    Volume scattering inside canopies: forests, tall crops. Drops when trees burn or are cut.
    Bright / magenta (HH)
    Double bounce: buildings, and water under trees (flooded forest) — an L-band speciality.
    Fringes
    Interferometry: each colour cycle is 11.9 cm of ground motion towards or away from the satellite.

    Polarisation: HH = sent horizontal, received horizontal; HV = sent horizontal, received vertical.

    Where NISAR goes next

    • Flood extent under cloud in near-real time, including flooded-forest alerts, for disaster agencies.
    • Paddy area and transplanting dates for food-security and crop-insurance programmes.
    • Deforestation alerts that smoke and cloud cannot block, plus L-band biomass-loss estimates.
    • InSAR for infrastructure: subsidence under railways, dams and coastal cities; landslide early warning.
    • Ice-sheet velocity every 12 days, feeding sea-level projections.
    • Dual-frequency science: S-band sees the canopy, L-band the trunks and ground beneath it.
